Key Takeaways


  1. What is skimming?
    We know that skimming is reading quickly to get the main idea, or gist, of a text without reading every word.

  2. What should we read when skimming a text?
    When skimming, we read the title, any headings, the first sentence of each paragraph, key words and numbers, and the final paragraph, which often sums up the text.

  3. What is a topic sentence?
    We know that a topic sentence is the sentence, often the first in a paragraph, that tells the reader what the whole paragraph is about.

  4. What is scanning?
    Scanning is moving our eyes quickly over a text to find one specific piece of information, while ignoring everything else.

  5. How do we know what to look for when scanning?
    We know that the question word tells us the shape of the answer: “when” points to a date or time, “how many” to a number, “who” to a name with a capital letter, and “where” to a place.

  6. What is the synonym trap?
    The synonym trap is when a question uses different words from the text to describe the same idea, so we must scan for the meaning and not only for the exact words in the question.

  7. What is fact extraction?
    We know that fact extraction is pulling out only the information that answers a specific question, and leaving out descriptions, feelings and opinions.

  8. When should a describing word be kept during fact extraction?
    A describing word should be dropped if it only adds colour or feeling, but kept if it changes the fact, such as a number, a size or a colour the question asks about.


Words that I Learnt from the Story 📚

Staysails
Meaning: Triangular sails set on stays (ropes or cables) running between masts or from a mast to the bow of a ship.
Example: The sailors unfurled the staysails to take advantage of the strengthening wind.

Bulwarks
Meaning: The raised sides of a ship above the deck that provide protection from waves and help prevent people from falling overboard.
Example: The crew braced themselves against the bulwarks as the vessel pitched in the storm.

Forecastle
Meaning: The forward part of a ship's upper deck, traditionally used by sailors as living quarters.
Example: Several crew members gathered on the forecastle to watch the coastline emerge from the morning mist.
